Assumptions and Limitations

The CLE screening models apply deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed internal-erosion risk analysis. They reduce input requirements and are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level. This chapter lists the assumptions the eight models share; each model's own manual lists the assumptions of its flaw assessment. Each assumption below states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

Event Tree​

  1. The SRP is the product of the eight node probabilities. Each node is the probability of its event given that every earlier node has occurred (see Event Tree).
    • Engineering basis: Event-tree convention for internal-erosion failure modes, following the node sequence of the concentrated leak erosion trees of the Typical Event Tree Database and the progression conditions of Fell et al. (2024) [?].
    • Effect on results: The SRP can be no larger than its smallest node, so a single low node — a filter, a cohesionless roof material, an effective cutoff — can control the curve.
    • When it may not hold: Where the nodes are strongly dependent in ways the conditional values do not capture, for example where the same soil property controls both initiation and roof support; a detailed analysis then assesses the joint condition directly.
  2. Intervention and breach are single values that apply at every pool level, defaulting to 1.0.
    • Engineering basis: Screening design decision (engineering judgment): taking no credit for intervention and treating breach as certain is the conservative screening posture, and site-specific values can be supplied.
    • Effect on results: With the defaults, the SRP is the probability of reaching the end of the progression nodes.
    • When it may not hold: Where monitoring and response time, or breach resistance, vary strongly with pool level; a pool-dependent assessment belongs in a detailed analysis.
  3. The curve is resolved on a grid of pool levels. The tree is evaluated at every supplied headwater value and at intermediate levels between them (see Event Tree).
    • Engineering basis: Screening simplification (engineering judgment): the nodes that step with pool level — at the bottom of a crest crack, the top of a filter, or the top of a wall — are located to within one grid interval.
    • Effect on results: A step between two grid levels appears in the curve as a change across that interval rather than at its exact elevation.
    • When it may not hold: Where the exact pool level of a step matters; supplying that pool level as a headwater value places a grid level on it.

Flaw​

  1. The flaw probability is the same at every pool level.
    • Engineering basis: Engineering judgment: the existence of a flaw is a condition of the embankment, not of the loading.
    • Effect on results: Pool dependence of the SRP comes entirely from initiation and, for the crest-crack models, from the pathway gates.
    • When it may not hold: Where cracking itself depends on pool level, such as hydraulic fracture under rising pool; that mechanism is outside the family.
  2. Qualitative site factors are converted to a flaw probability by risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring and a log-linear curve. This applies to every model except cross-valley differential settlement (see Flaw Conventions).
    • Engineering basis: Fell et al. (2024) [?] for the scoring scheme; the interpolation form is requested at marker IN-03 in Flaw Conventions.
    • Effect on results: The flaw probability moves by roughly constant factors per step of the weighted sum, and takes the end value beyond the tabulated range.
    • When it may not hold: Where site investigation supports a flaw probability more directly than the factor scores do; the risk team's estimate then replaces the screening value.
  3. Three compaction models multiply the scored probability by a flaw-continuity probability keyed to their compaction factor.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-04 in Flaw Conventions.
    • Effect on results: The best compaction rating (a likelihood factor of 1) reduces the flaw probability to negligible, and each step toward poorer compaction above a likelihood factor of 2 raises it by one to two orders of magnitude. The compaction category enters the flaw probability twice — through its weight in the weighted sum and again through the flaw-continuity probability — so the flaw probability is especially sensitive to that selection.
    • When it may not hold: Where compaction records conflict with field performance, such as observed seepage through an embankment rated well compacted; the observation override then applies.
  4. An observed flaw has a flaw probability of 1.0.
    • Engineering basis: Engineering judgment: an observation of leakage, wet spots, or continuous cracking demonstrates the flaw, so its probability is not estimated from site factors.
    • Effect on results: The SRP is governed entirely by the downstream nodes.
    • When it may not hold: Where the observation has another explanation, such as surface cracking that is not continuous or seepage along a different path; the override should be reserved for observations judged to demonstrate the modeled flaw.
  5. The base of a core or abutment compaction flaw is placed one third of the way from the normal pool to the crest, unless an observed leakage elevation (core) replaces it.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in Flaw Conventions.
    • Effect on results: Initiation cannot occur, and the SRP is negligible, at pool levels at or below this elevation, and walls are judged against it. Filter presence is stated by the user, not compared with this elevation.
    • When it may not hold: Where construction records or performance locate the poorly compacted zone; an observed leakage elevation should then be used where the model accepts one.

Initiation​

  1. Initiation in a crest crack is certain once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack, and zero at or below it. Tailwater plays no part.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-06 in Initiation.
    • Effect on results: The crack depth alone sets the pool level at which the crest-crack SRP rises from negligible; no shear stress analysis can lower the initiation probability above that level (conservative).
    • When it may not hold: For narrow cracks in highly erosion-resistant soils, or cracks that close or fill below the surface; a crack-geometry initiation analysis then applies.
  2. A poorly compacted zone is idealized as a cylindrical pipe of uncertain initial diameter at the base of the flaw.
    • Engineering basis: The cylindrical-pipe idealization of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox (see Initiation); the basis of the default diameter ranges is requested at marker IN-11 in Initiation.
    • Effect on results: The shear stress rises in proportion to the diameter, so the initial diameter range strongly shapes the initiation curve.
    • When it may not hold: Where the flaw is better represented as a crack, such as a gap against a structure; the toolbox crack geometries then apply.
  3. The default critical shear stress is a triangular distribution over a range set by the soil classification.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see markers IN-07 and IN-10 in Initiation.
    • Effect on results: Soils of one classification share one erodibility range regardless of compaction, water content, or dispersivity.
    • When it may not hold: For dispersive soils, soils whose erodibility differs markedly from their classification's range, or where erosion testing is available; a site-specific distribution should then be supplied.
  4. The compaction mechanisms are not modeled in clean or nearly clean sands and gravels — the classes SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP, with less than 12 percent fines.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-09 in Initiation.
    • Effect on results: A core of SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, or GP cannot be analyzed by the compaction models. Silty soils (SM, GM, ML) are analyzed even where they are non-plastic.
    • When it may not hold: Not applicable within the family; other internal-erosion failure modes address such soils.
  5. The seepage path is a horizontal line at the base of the flaw across the core section, or across the shell when no core is described.
    • Engineering basis: The pipe-length measurement of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox, which measures across the impervious core of a zoned embankment with very permeable outer zones and across the embankment slopes of a homogeneous one.
    • Effect on results: The whole net head is lost along the measured length; a shorter length gives a steeper gradient and a higher initiation probability.
    • When it may not hold: For sloping cores, flaws not perpendicular to the centerline, or outer zones that are not free draining; a supplied seepage path length should then be used.
  6. The critical shear stress and the initial pipe diameter are uncertain independently, and each realization holds its values at every pool level.
    • Engineering basis: The probabilistic method of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox, which samples each from its own distribution.
    • Effect on results: The initiation curve never falls where the gradient rises with pool level (it is non-decreasing there).
    • When it may not hold: Where the two are correlated, for example where a more erodible soil also forms larger initial openings. Under such positive dependence the ratio of diameter to critical shear stress has a wider spread, so the initiation curve rises over a wider range of pool levels than the independent treatment shows.

Failure Pathway and Progression​

  1. On a crest crack, continuation and roof support share one gate at the top of the first filter the pathway meets. Above that elevation continuation is certain and the full-pathway materials govern roof support; at or below it the continuation method and the below-filter materials apply; a filter reaching the crest with no full-pathway materials given applies at every pool level.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in Failure Pathway and Progression.
    • Effect on results: A crest-crack curve with a filter steps where the pool crosses the filter's top. It steps up when the continuation probability PceP_{ce}Pce​ is smaller than the ratio of full-pathway to below-filter roof support, and down when it is larger (see Node 3: Continuation).
    • When it may not hold: Where a filter's top varies along the crack, or a second filter lies further downstream on the full pathway; the first filter's top is used throughout.
  2. On a compaction flaw, the pathway, its filter, its materials, and the walls that reach it are fixed by the flaw elevation. Continuation, roof support, and flow limitation are each one value at every pool level.
    • Engineering basis: Engineering judgment: the flaw is at one elevation, so what it meets does not change with the pool.
    • Effect on results: Only initiation varies with pool level.
    • When it may not hold: Where the poorly compacted zone extends over a range of elevations; the flaw elevation should then be chosen as the one that governs.
  3. Roof support is the minimum roof-support probability across the materials on the pathway.
    • Engineering basis: Fell et al. (2024) [?] for the probabilities by soil classification; engineering judgment for the minimum, since every material along the pathway must hold a roof.
    • Effect on results: One cohesionless material on the pathway reduces the SRP by up to two orders of magnitude.
    • When it may not hold: Where the governing material occupies only a short, well-confined length of the pathway.
  4. Crack filling depends only on the embankment type.
    • Engineering basis: Fell et al. (2024) [?]; the meaning of the values is requested at marker IN-13 in Failure Pathway and Progression.
    • Effect on results: Only a central-core earth and rockfill embankment receives credit, which halves its SRP.
    • When it may not hold: Where the gradation of the upstream zone is known to be able, or unable, to fill the pipe.
  5. Flow limitation is the minimum across the applicable upstream elements, and a wall limits flow only below its top.
    • Engineering basis: Fell et al. (2024) [?] for the probabilities; the gating conventions are requested at marker IN-14 in Failure Pathway and Progression.
    • Effect on results: A modern cutoff wall that reaches the flaw reduces the SRP by three orders of magnitude; above its top, a crest-crack curve loses that reduction.
    • When it may not hold: Where a wall is discontinuous, damaged, or does not span the flaw laterally.
  6. The conduit compaction model takes no flow-limitation credit.
    • Engineering basis: Basis requested — see marker IN-14 in Failure Pathway and Progression.
    • Effect on results: An upstream cutoff or facing does not reduce the SRP of a conduit flaw (conservative).
    • When it may not hold: Where an upstream element demonstrably limits flow into the zone around the conduit.
