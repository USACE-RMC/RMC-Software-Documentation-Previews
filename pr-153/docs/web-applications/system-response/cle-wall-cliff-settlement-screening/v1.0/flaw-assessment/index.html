Flaw Assessment

The flaw assessment answers two questions about the transverse crack that differential settlement against the wall or cliff can open near the crest. How likely is a continuous crack through the embankment (Node 1 of the event tree)? And how deep is it, which sets the pool level above which the crack can carry a concentrated leak (Node 2)? Both answers come from the same three quantities — the bench-width ratio, the normalized settlement, and the abutment slope below the wall or cliff — and both are the same at every pool level. The calculation proceeds in the order of this chapter:

  1. Form the bench-width ratio and the normalized settlement.
  2. Score the three factors as likelihood factors and form their weighted sum.
  3. Convert the weighted sum to the flaw probability.
  4. Compute the crack width.
  5. Compute the effective wall height and the crack depth, and from the crack depth the bottom-of-crack elevation.

Steps 2 and 3 apply the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring scheme shared by seven of the CLE screening models, documented in the Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring section of the common methods manual; this chapter documents the factors, bands, and curve of this model. The flaw probability is used directly as Node 1: the model applies no flaw-continuity stage and no observation override.

Normalized Ratios​

Bench-width ratio​

The bench-width ratio compares the width of the bench at the base of the wall or cliff with the height of the wall or cliff:

rb=WbHwr_b = \frac{W_b}{H_w}rb​=Hw​Wb​​

where WbW_bWb​ is the bench width and HwH_wHw​ is the wall or cliff height. A ratio of zero describes fill placed directly against the wall or cliff with no bench; a large ratio describes a wide bench that sets the fill well back from the wall or cliff.

Normalized settlement​

The normalized settlement rsr_srs​ is the post-construction settlement expressed as a percentage of the dam height. When the maximum post-construction settlement sss is supplied,

rs=100 sHr_s = 100\,\frac{s}{H}rs​=100Hs​

with sss and HHH in the same length unit. When no settlement is supplied, the normalized settlement takes a default value set by the year of construction YYY:

rs={0.5 %Y<19500.25 %Y≥1950r_s = \begin{cases} 0.5\ \% & Y < 1950 \\[4pt] 0.25\ \% & Y \ge 1950 \end{cases}rs​={0.5 %0.25 %​Y<1950Y≥1950​

A dam completed in 1950 itself takes the 0.25 percent default. A supplied settlement always takes precedence over the default, whatever the year.

Likelihood Factors​

Each of the three flaw factors is scored as a likelihood factor from 1, the condition least likely to produce a continuous crack, to 4, the most likely, by placing its input in one of four bands (Table). Each factor carries a risk factor that weights its score (Table), following Fell et al. (2024) [?]:

  • Cross-valley profile — scored on the bench-width ratio rbr_brb​. A narrow bench, or none, scores high; a wide bench scores low.
  • Settlement — scored on the normalized settlement rsr_srs​. Larger settlement relative to the dam height scores higher.
  • Abutment slope — scored on the abutment slope below the wall or cliff β1\beta_1β1​. A steeper slope scores higher.

The cross-valley profile bands differ: the band for a likelihood factor of 2 includes its upper limit of 2.5, the band for 3 includes both of its limits, 0.25 and 1, and the band for 4 excludes its upper limit of 0.25. Weighted sum​

The three scores combine into the weighted sum defined in the Weighted Sum section of the common methods manual:

Σ=3 LFxv+2 LFs+1 LFβ\Sigma = 3\,LF_{xv} + 2\,LF_{s} + 1\,LF_{\beta}Σ=3LFxv​+2LFs​+1LFβ​

where LFxvLF_{xv}LFxv​, LFsLF_{s}LFs​, and LFβLF_{\beta}LFβ​ are the likelihood factors of the cross-valley profile, settlement, and abutment slope. Every likelihood factor of this model is a whole number, so the weighted sum is a whole number between 6 and 24.

Incompatible-condition factors​

The cross-valley profile and the abutment slope are incompatible-condition factors (see the Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum section of the common methods manual). When either scores 1 — a bench wider than 2.5 times the wall or cliff height, or an abutment slope of 25° or flatter — the flaw probability is zero whatever the other two factors score. The settlement factor is not an incompatible-condition factor: a settlement score of 1 lowers the weighted sum but does not by itself rule out a crack.

Once neither geometric factor scores 1, each scores at least 2, so every weighted sum that reaches the flaw-probability curve lies between 10 and 24, and every whole number in that range can occur.

INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Engineering Basis

The rule that a cross-valley profile score of 1 (bench-width ratio greater than 2.5) or an abutment slope score of 1 (slope of 25° or flatter) forces the flaw probability to zero, while a settlement score of 1 does not, requires an engineering basis. Provide the literature source (the table or section of Fell et al. (2024), if it is the source) or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): a bench that wide sets the fill far enough from the wall or cliff, and an abutment that flat gives a gradual enough transition, that the geometry cannot concentrate differential settlement into a continuous transverse crack, whereas some settlement is always present. Confirm or replace.

No Observation Override​

The model has no observation override: an observed transverse crack at the crest does not set the flaw probability to 1.0, and no observation is taken as an input. The flaw probability is always the scored value. The common methods manual lists the overrides of the other models in its Observation Overrides section.

INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Engineering Basis

The absence of an observation override in this model — where the cross-valley settlement, desiccation, and frost-cracking models each accept an observation of continuous transverse cracking that sets the flaw probability to 1.0 — requires an engineering basis or a documentation decision to state here. State why an observed crack adjacent to the wall or cliff is not used to override the scored probability, and what a user who has observed such a crack should do.

The crack width is a supporting result. It does not enter the crack depth or any node of the event tree: in this model, initiation depends only on whether the pool stands above the bottom of the crack (see Event Tree).

At a bench-width ratio of exactly 2.5 the crack width is zero at every settlement and slope, and between ratios of 2 and 2.5 it falls toward zero. The other results do not follow it to zero. A ratio of 2.5 still scores a cross-valley profile likelihood factor of 2, so the flaw probability is not zero unless the abutment slope scores 1, and the effective wall height ratio is held at its value for a ratio of 2, so the crack depth is not zero either. Initiation is then certain above the bottom of a crack that the width relationship gives as zero width.

Bottom of the Crack​

The bottom-of-crack elevation is the crest elevation less the crack depth, as for every crest-crack model. The crack depth is measured down from the crest whatever the elevation of the top of the wall or cliff; the model takes no top-of-wall or top-of-cliff elevation (see marker IN-01 in Input Parameters). The convention is documented in the Crest-Crack Models: Bottom of the Crack section of the common methods manual:

zbc=zc−dcz_{bc} = z_c - d_czbc​=zc​−dc​

It is the initiation gate of the event tree, and the elevation at which the below-filter roof materials are taken (see Event Tree). It is computed whenever the inputs are valid, including when an incompatible-condition factor forces the flaw probability to zero; the SRP is then negligible at every pool level regardless of the crack depth.

Range of the Tabulated Relationships​

The crack width, effective wall height, and crack depth relationships are tabulated over normalized settlements from 0 to 2 percent, bench-width ratios from 0 to 2.5 (0 to 2 for the effective wall height), and abutment slopes from 15° to 60°. Beyond the tabulated range of an input, each relationship takes its value at the nearest tabulated edge of that input; it is not extrapolated. In particular:

  • A normalized settlement above 2 percent gives the crack width and depth of 2 percent: the crack depth is the full effective wall height.
  • An abutment slope steeper than 60° gives the values of 60°, and a slope flatter than 15° gives the values of 15° (such a slope also forces the flaw probability to zero through the abutment slope factor).
  • A bench-width ratio above 2 gives the effective wall height ratio of 2; above 2.5 the cross-valley profile factor also forces the flaw probability to zero.

The likelihood-factor bands of Table have no such limit: their outer bands are open-ended.
