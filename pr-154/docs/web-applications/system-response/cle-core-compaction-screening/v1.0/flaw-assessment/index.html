Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability PflawP_{flaw}Pflaw​ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted soil exists through the embankment core. The flaw assessment also fixes the base-of-flaw elevation zfz_fzf​, which sets the pipe invert of the initiation analysis (Node 2) and is the elevation the top of any upstream wall is compared with (Node 6); the other nodes do not use it. The assessment is deterministic and does not depend on pool level, so PflawP_{flaw}Pflaw​ and zfz_fzf​ are the same at every headwater.

The assessment follows the conventions shared by the CLE family, documented in the Flaw Conventions chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. It runs in two stages:

  1. Scoring — three factors are scored and combined into a weighted sum, which is converted to a raw flaw probability PrawP_{raw}Praw​.
  2. Flaw continuity — PrawP_{raw}Praw​ is multiplied by a flaw-continuity probability PcfP_{cf}Pcf​ keyed to the compaction score, giving PflawP_{flaw}Pflaw​.

An observation of embankment leakage or wet spots overrides both stages and the base-of-flaw heuristic (see Observation Override).

Compaction factor​

The likelihood factor of the core compaction factor, LFcompLF_{comp}LFcomp​, is set by the core compaction category (Table). The table also gives the flaw-continuity probability PcfP_{cf}Pcf​ that the same score selects in the second stage (see Flaw Continuity).

No category carries a likelihood factor of 2. The Unknown category scores 3.5, between Poorly compacted and Very poorly compacted, so selecting Unknown gives a higher flaw probability than selecting Poorly compacted.

The compaction likelihood factors of Table (Well compacted 1, Poorly compacted 3, Very poorly compacted 4, Unknown 3.5) require an engineering basis. State the source of the values, why no category scores 2, and why Unknown is scored at 3.5 — above Poorly compacted — rather than at a central value. Provide the table of Fell et al. (2024) they are taken from, or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): Unknown is scored toward the poorly compacted end of the scale as a conservative screening posture, so that a core whose compaction cannot be judged is not credited as better compacted than the evidence supports. Confirm or replace.

Continuity factor​

The continuity factor scores the dimensions of the embankment section through which a poorly compacted zone would have to run. Its likelihood factor LFcontLF_{cont}LFcont​ is the larger of two banded scores, one of the base width WWW and one of the width-to-height ratio W/HW/HW/H:

LFcont=max⁡(LFW, LFW/H)LF_{cont} = \max\left(LF_W,\ LF_{W/H}\right)LFcont​=max(LFW​, LFW/H​)

where WWW and HHH are the base width and height of the embankment section — the core section when one is supplied, otherwise the shell — or the user-supplied flaw base width and flaw height (see Input Parameters). The section height HHH is the quantity written HsH_sHs​ in Section Geometry Used in Scoring in the CLE Common Methods manual. The base width is in the length unit of the elevations. Table lists the bands.

Table: Continuity likelihood factor bands; the larger of the two scores governs.
Likelihood FactorBase Width WWidth-to-Height Ratio W/H
1Greater than 50Greater than 2

Taking the larger of the two scores means that either a narrow section or a slender one is enough to make a continuous flaw more likely: a section scores 1 only when it is both wider than 50 and more than twice as wide as it is high. A band boundary itself belongs to the band below it; for example, a base width of exactly 50 scores 2.

The continuity bands of Table (base width boundaries 50, 30, and 15; width-to-height boundaries 2, 0.75, and 0.25) and the rule that the larger of the two scores governs (Equation) require an engineering basis. Provide the source table or the engineering-judgment attribution, and state the unit of the base-width boundaries: the model applies them to the base width in whatever length unit the elevations use, with no conversion.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the base-width boundaries are in feet, so a user working in meters should convert the section before running the model. Confirm or replace.

Borrow variability factor​

The borrow variability factor has a fixed likelihood factor LFvar=2.5LF_{var} = 2.5LFvar​=2.5. It is not a user selection and does not vary between sites.

INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Engineering Basis

The borrow variability factor and its fixed likelihood factor of 2.5 require a definition and an engineering basis. State what the factor represents physically (for example, the variability of the borrow material placed in the core, which can leave zones of more erodible or less compactable soil), why it is fixed rather than selected by the user, and the basis for the value of 2.5, between likelihood factors 2 and 3.

Weighted Sum and Raw Flaw Probability​

The three scores combine into the weighted sum

Σ=3 LFcomp+2 LFcont+1 LFvar\Sigma = 3\,LF_{comp} + 2\,LF_{cont} + 1\,LF_{var}Σ=3LFcomp​+2LFcont​+1LFvar​

with LFvar=2.5LF_{var} = 2.5LFvar​=2.5, which ranges from 7.5 (well compacted, continuity score 1) to 22.5 (very poorly compacted, continuity score 4). The raw flaw probability PrawP_{raw}Praw​ is interpolated from Table, linearly in the logarithm of the probability between tabulated points, as described in Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum in the CLE Common Methods manual.

Every attainable weighted sum lies inside the tabulated range, so the end values are never held. Because the lowest point carries a negligible probability, the raw probability falls steeply below a weighted sum of 9; at the lowest attainable sum of 7.5 it is several orders of magnitude below 1 × 10-4. That range matters only for a well-compacted core, whose flaw probability is negligible after the flaw-continuity stage in any case. The model applies no lower threshold and no incompatible-condition factor, so no score forces PrawP_{raw}Praw​ to zero.

Flaw Continuity​

The second stage multiplies the raw probability by the flaw-continuity probability PcfP_{cf}Pcf​, interpolated log-linearly from the compaction score on the curve the model shares with the wall or cliff and the foundation or abutment compaction models (see Flaw-Continuity Stage in the CLE Common Methods manual):

Pflaw=Praw(Σ)×Pcf(LFcomp)P_{flaw} = P_{raw}(\Sigma) \times P_{cf}(LF_{comp})Pflaw​=Praw​(Σ)×Pcf​(LFcomp​)

The value for each category is listed in Table. The Unknown score of 3.5 is interpolated between the points at 3 and 4, giving 1 × 10-2. The point of the curve at a score of 2 (1 × 10-4) is not used by this model, because no category scores 2.

The compaction category therefore enters the flaw probability twice: through its weight of 3 in the weighted sum, and again through PcfP_{cf}Pcf​. The section geometry enters only through the weighted sum.

Because borrow variability is fixed, the flaw probability depends only on the compaction category and the continuity score. Table lists every combination.

A well-compacted core has a negligible flaw probability whatever the section geometry, because its flaw-continuity probability is negligible. For the other categories, each step in the continuity score raises the flaw probability by a factor of about 2 to 3.5, while moving from Poorly compacted to Very poorly compacted raises it by a factor of about 350 to 600, with Unknown in between.

Example​

An embankment section with a base width of 350 and a height of 60 (from its base at elevation 205 to a crest at elevation 265), a core of Unknown compaction, and a normal pool at elevation 235 scores as follows:

  • W=350W = 350W=350 is greater than 50, so LFW=1LF_W = 1LFW​=1; W/H=5.83W/H = 5.83W/H=5.83 is greater than 2, so LFW/H=1LF_{W/H} = 1LFW/H​=1; hence LFcont=1LF_{cont} = 1LFcont​=1.
  • Unknown compaction gives LFcomp=3.5LF_{comp} = 3.5LFcomp​=3.5.
  • Σ=3×3.5+2×1+2.5=15\Sigma = 3 \times 3.5 + 2 \times 1 + 2.5 = 15Σ=3×3.5+2×1+2.5=15.
  • Between the tabulated points at 13 and 18, Praw=2×10−3×(20)0.4≈6.6×10−3P_{raw} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \times (20)^{0.4} \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-3}Praw​=2×10−3×(20)0.4≈6.6×10−3.
  • Pcf(3.5)=1×10−2P_{cf}(3.5) = 1 \times 10^{-2}Pcf​(3.5)=1×10−2, so Pflaw≈6.6×10−5P_{flaw} \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-5}Pflaw​≈6.6×10−5.

Observation Override​

When embankment leakage or wet spots are reported as observed, the flaw is taken as demonstrated:

Pflaw=1.0,zf=zlkP_{flaw} = 1.0, \qquad z_f = z_{lk}Pflaw​=1.0,zf​=zlk​

where zlkz_{lk}zlk​ is the observed leakage or wet-spot elevation. The override replaces the scored probability and the normal-pool heuristic together. It is the only CLE compaction override that also moves the flaw elevation (see Observation Overrides in the CLE Common Methods manual). If a compaction category is supplied with the observation, the factors are still scored and reported, but they do not affect the result; without a category, no scores are reported. The override sets only Node 1 and the flaw elevation: every downstream node is evaluated as usual at the observed elevation.

INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Definition

State which elevation the user should enter as the leakage or wet-spot elevation zlkz_{lk}zlk​ — for example, the elevation of the observed seepage exit on the downstream face, or the lowest observed wet spot when several are observed — and the basis for taking it as the invert of a horizontal pipe through the core. The pipe is analyzed at this elevation from the upstream face to the downstream face, so an exit that the flaw reaches by a sloping path would place the pipe at the exit elevation rather than at the elevation of the flaw within the core.

Base of the Flaw​

Without an observation, the base of the flaw is placed by the normal-pool heuristic shared with the abutment case of the foundation or abutment compaction model, one third of the way from the normal pool elevation znpz_{np}znp​ to the crest elevation zcz_czc​:

zf=znp+13 (zc−znp)z_f = z_{np} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,\bigl(z_c - z_{np}\bigr)zf​=znp​+31​(zc​−znp​)

The heuristic is documented in Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw in the CLE Common Methods manual. The upper reference is always the crest of the embankment, even when a core section with a lower top is supplied.

Whichever way it is resolved, the base of the flaw is the invert of the pipe in the initiation analysis and must lie within the embankment section used for initiation: at or below the top of that section (the core top when a core section is supplied, otherwise the crest), at or above the lowest segment bottom of each face, and at a positive elevation. An analysis whose base of the flaw falls outside these limits — for example, a core top below the heuristic elevation — is not evaluated, because its inputs are inconsistent.
