Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability PflawP_{flaw}Pflaw​ that a continuous transverse frost crack exists across the crest. The flaw assessment also produces the one flaw quantity the downstream nodes use — the bottom-of-crack elevation zbcz_{bc}zbc​, below which water cannot enter the crack — and a crack width reported for reference. The assessment follows the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of Fell et al. (2024) [?], in the form shared by the CLE screening family and documented in Flaw Conventions. It proceeds in five steps:

  1. Score the three site factors — climate, crest soil, and surface cover — each with a likelihood factor.
  2. Form the weighted sum of the likelihood factors.
  3. Convert the weighted sum to a raw flaw probability, applying the lower threshold.
  4. Apply the observation override to obtain PflawP_{flaw}Pflaw​.
  5. Take the crack depth from the climate and the bottom of the crack from the crest elevation less that depth; take the crack width from the weighted sum.

A preliminary check precedes the scoring: when every listed crest soil is one that cannot frost-crack, the mechanism does not apply and the assessment ends there (see Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack).

Each factor jjj carries a risk factor RFjRF_jRFj​, its weight, and a likelihood factor LFjLF_jLFj​, its score for the site on a scale on which 1 is the least likely condition for a frost crack and 4 the most likely (Table). The likelihood-factor tables follow Fell et al. (2024) [?].

None of the three factors is an incompatible-condition factor in the sense of the common scoring scheme: a likelihood factor of 1 does not by itself force the flaw probability to zero. Only the climate can score 1, and an above-freezing climate is instead limited by its shallow crack (see Crack Depth and Bottom of the Crack).

Weighted Sum​

The three scores combine into the weighted sum of Flaw Conventions:

Σ=3 LFclim+2 LFcore+1 LFsurf\Sigma = 3\,LF_{clim} + 2\,LF_{core} + 1\,LF_{surf}Σ=3LFclim​+2LFcore​+1LFsurf​

The weighted sum is always a whole number, because the one fractional score (3.5, for CL-ML) carries a risk factor of 2. It ranges from 9 — an above-freezing climate, a fat clay (CH) crest, and a paved surface — to 24 — the longest freeze duration, a clayey gravel (GC) or high-plasticity silt (MH) crest, and no road or pathway. Because the climate carries the largest weight, one step in the climate category moves the weighted sum by 3, one step in the crest soil by 2 (or 1 for the half step to or from CL-ML), and one step in the surface cover by 1.

Observation Override​

When continuous frost cracking has been observed (see Input Parameters), the flaw probability is 1.0. Otherwise it is the raw probability, never less than a negligibly small value:

Pflaw={1.0continuous frost cracking observedmax⁡(Praw,ε)otherwiseP_{flaw} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{continuous frost cracking observed} \\[4pt] \max\bigl(P_{raw}, \varepsilon\bigr) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}Pflaw​={1.0max(Praw​,ε)​continuous frost cracking observedotherwise​

where ε\varepsilonε is the negligibly small probability the CLE family uses in place of zero (see SRP Computation). The override takes precedence over the lower threshold: an observed continuous frost crack has a flaw probability of 1.0 even where the weighted sum is below 13. It sets Node 1 only — the crack depth and every downstream node are evaluated as usual — and it applies only when the mechanism applies, as described in Observation Overrides.

Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack​

The six clean and nearly clean sand and gravel classes — SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP, the classes with less than 12 percent fines — do not frost-crack and are not scored. A listed class of this group is set aside, and the remaining listed classes govern by Equation. When every listed class belongs to this group, the mechanism does not apply, as described in Inapplicable Mechanism:

  • the flaw probability is the negligible value ε\varepsilonε, and no weighted sum is formed;
  • the observation override is not consulted, since observed cracking in such a crest cannot be frost cracking;
  • no crack forms, so the crack depth and crack width are zero and the bottom of the crack is the crest elevation;
  • initiation is zero at every pool level, and the SRP is negligible throughout.

The silty classes SM, GM, and ML are scored, although they can be non-plastic.

INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis

The treatment of SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP as crest soils that cannot frost-crack — set aside when other classes are listed, and making the mechanism inapplicable when they are the only classes listed — requires an engineering basis, including why the silty classes SM, GM, and ML, which can also be non-plastic, are scored. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): a cohesionless soil cannot sustain an open frost crack, so these classes are absent from the core-material table of Fell et al. (2024). Confirm or replace.

Crack Depth and Bottom of the Crack​

The crack depth dcd_cdc​ depends on the climate category alone (Table); it does not depend on the crest soil, the surface cover, or the weighted sum. The bottom of the crack follows from Crest-crack models: bottom of the crack:

zbc=zc−dcz_{bc} = z_c - d_czbc​=zc​−dc​

where zcz_czc​ is the crest elevation. Initiation is possible only at pool levels above zbcz_{bc}zbc​ (see Event Tree). When the mechanism applies, the bottom of the crack is computed this way whatever the flaw probability, including when the override applies or the weighted sum is below the threshold. When it does not apply, the crack depth is zero and the bottom of the crack is the crest elevation (see Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack).

The shallow 1 ft crack of an above-freezing climate is what limits that climate's contribution: the flaw probability of an above-freezing site can be non-negligible (up to 4.6×10−34.6 \times 10^{-3}4.6×10−3 from the scored factors, at a weighted sum of 15), but the SRP can rise from negligible only for pools higher than 1 ft below the crest.

The tabulated depths are in feet. They are subtracted from the crest elevation as given, with no unit conversion.

INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Applicability

The crack depths of Table are in feet and are subtracted from the crest elevation with no conversion, so an analysis in which elevations are entered in meters would place the bottom of the crack 6, 4, 2, or 1 m below the crest — about 3.3 times too deep. State whether the model is to be used only with elevations in feet, or how a user working in SI units should proceed, so that the limitation can be stated here. (The family-level question is marker IN-01 of the common methods manual.)

INPUT NEEDED IN-07 — Engineering Basis

The crack depths by climate category (Table: 6, 4, 2, and 1 ft), and their independence from the crest soil and the surface cover, require an engineering basis. Provide the literature source (for example, a frost-penetration or frost-crack-depth relationship) or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the depths represent the depth of frost penetration into the crest for each freeze duration, over which frost cracks can extend. Confirm or replace.

The model also reports a crack width wcw_cwc​, interpolated linearly (not logarithmically) in the weighted sum between the points of Table, following the crack-width relationship of Fell et al. (2024) [?]. A weighted sum at or below 12 gives zero width, and a sum of 24 gives the largest width, 20 mm. With a weighted sum of 14, for example, the crack width is 3 mm.

The crack width is reported for reference only. It does not enter the bottom of the crack, the initiation node, or the SRP, since initiation in an open crest crack is taken as certain without a shear stress analysis. It can inform the crack opening assumed in a detailed crack-geometry initiation analysis.
