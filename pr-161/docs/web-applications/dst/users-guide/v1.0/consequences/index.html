Dam Screening Tool

Consequences

The Consequences module estimates what happens downstream when the dam fails, and when it does not. The screening team builds a two-dimensional hydraulic model of the area below the dam, simulates the flood at each loading level with HEC-RAS, and then simulates life loss and economic damages in each flood with LifeSim. The results become the consequence functions that the Risk Analysis module combines with the hazard and system response (see Chapter 32, Risk Analysis).

The module has six pages, reached from Consequences in the module navigation, in the order the work is done:

  • Inundation Geometry builds the hydraulic model: its area, dam, rivers, terrain, land cover and mesh.
  • Inundation Analysis sets the loading levels, boundary conditions and breach of each analysis, and runs the flood simulations.
  • Consequence Data holds the structure inventory, the Emergency Response ratings and an optional non-failure hazard zone.
  • Consequence Analysis sets when each hazard occurs and how much notice people have, and runs the consequence simulations.
  • Results Map shows the consequences of one loading level on the map, structure by structure.
  • Results Data shows each consequence function as graphs and tables and holds functions entered by hand.

None of these pages is verified. Their inputs are read-only to users who are not on the screening team and while the screening's inputs are locked (see Input Lock). The methods behind the simulations are described in the HEC-RAS and LifeSim documentation and in the DST Technical Reference Manual.

Inundation Geometry​

The Inundation Geometry page, at Consequences > Inundation Geometry, defines the hydraulic model: the area it covers, the dam, the rivers and other lines that shape the mesh, the terrain and land cover, and the computational mesh built from them.

The map​

Every page of the Consequences module except Consequence Analysis and Results Data is a map. The toolbar at the upper right changes the map style, identifies the features under the pointer (Identify features), measures distances, zooms in, out and to all layers, resets the bearing to north and exaggerates the terrain. When a feature that can be edited is selected, the toolbar adds Edit selected feature, and while editing, Save edits, Cancel edits (Escape) and Add points. The strip under the left panel shows the pointer's longitude, latitude and zoom, and hints for the current tool, such as "Click a feature to select".

The legend groups the layers as Inundation Geometry, Consequence Data, Inundation Results and Reference Layers, which include USGS rivers and lakes and the levees of the National Levee Database. DST remembers which layers are on for each page during the session. Below zoom 13 the map shows a sample of the structures; zooming in shows every one.

Model Configuration panel​

The add buttons, in order, are Dam, Model Area, River, Coastline, Breakline, Refinement, Terrain and Land Cover, followed by Mesh; resting the pointer on a button gives the feature's full name. A screening has one model area, one dam centerline, one terrain and one land cover; once one exists, its button replaces it ("Replace the …").

The tree lists Model Geometry, with the mesh status, the mesh button and a download button, and under it the Dam Centerline, Model Area, River Centerlines, Coastlines, Breaklines and Refinement Regions, then Terrains and Land Covers, each with a count. Each row has a download button. An amber warning icon marks a feature whose settings are incomplete or invalid; resting the pointer on it gives the reason.

Adding a line or area​

The model area, dam centerline, river centerlines, coastlines, breaklines and refinement regions are added through one dialog, titled with the feature's name:

  1. 1The user selects the feature's add button and enters its fields (listed in the sections below).
  2. 2Optionally, the user selects Import Geometry from File to read the outline from a GeoJSON (.geojson, .json) or KML (.kml) file. A river can instead be picked with Pick a River on the Map, and a breakline with Pick a Levee on the Map or Pick a Road on the Map. A preview sketches the new feature over the existing model in gray.
  3. 3The user selects Create (or Replace). When no geometry was given, DST creates the feature and starts the map's drawing tool for it at once.

A picked river comes from USGS NHDPlus V2, and only its stretch through the model area, upstream to downstream, is kept. A picked levee comes from the USACE National Levee Database, and a picked road from OpenStreetMap (motorway, trunk, primary or secondary roads). Picking needs a model area. With the reference layers on, selecting a river or levee on the map also offers Import as River Centerline or Import as Breakline. Before the line is added, a preview shows its length and any notes, such as "It was trimmed at the dam: only the stretch downstream of the dam is kept.", with Pick Again and Use This River, Use This Levee or Use This Road.

Terrain​

The terrain supplies the ground elevations of the mesh. Terrain opens the Add Terrain dialog, with two tabs:

  • OpenTopography downloads a terrain for the model geometry with a 660 ft (200 m) buffer. The Dataset is one of Copernicus 30m (COP30, the default) or 90m (COP90), SRTM 30m (SRTMGL1) or 90m (SRTMGL3), or USGS 1m, 10m or 30m (3DEP, United States only). A Download Area preview shows the rectangles to be requested. One download covers at most 4° of latitude or longitude, 1.5 billion cells and 40 requests. Download starts it, and it continues after the dialog closes.
  • Upload File reads a GeoTIFF (.tif, .tiff) digital elevation model up to 11 GB, chosen with Select GeoTIFF File. Elevation Units (Meters or Feet) is required; meters are converted to feet.

Name is optional in both; a blank name is generated. While the terrain processes, its row shows a spinner, and DST reports "Terrain "[name]" is ready!" when it finishes. Its properties panel shows its name, source, resolution, elevation units, dataset, import date and bounding box, and warns when the model geometry extends past the terrain.

Land cover​

The land cover sets the roughness (Manning's n) of the floodplain. Land Cover opens the Add Land Cover dialog, with two tabs:

  • NLCD downloads the National Land Cover Database for the model geometry with a 660 ft buffer. NLCD Year is Latest available (the default) or a release from 2021 back to 2001.
  • Upload File reads a GeoTIFF of whole-number land cover classes. NLCD codes keep their NLCD names and Manning's n; any other code becomes "Class N" with n = 0.06 until it is set.

The properties panel shows the land cover's source, year, resolution and bounding box, and Edit Manning's n… opens the Manning's n by Land Cover Class dialog. It lists each class's Code, Land Cover Class, Default n and Manning's n, which is editable, greater than 0 and at most 1. Reset to defaults restores the defaults and Save keeps the changes. Changing a value marks existing simulation results as out of date. Where the model extends past the land cover, the mesh uses a Manning's n of 0.06.

Computational mesh​

Mesh computes the computational mesh from the model geometry, and selecting it again while the mesh computes cancels it. The status under Model Geometry reads "Queued…", a percentage, "[N] cells", "[N] cells · [K] warnings", "Failed", "Cancelled" or "Not computed". A mesh needs a model area with a drawn boundary.

When the mesh fails or has warnings, the Mesh failed or Mesh warnings card opens at the top of the map. It lists each message from the mesher with Show on Map, which outlines and zooms to the problem. DST does not recompute the mesh when the geometry changes; the mesh must be recomputed after the geometry or the dam's settings change.

Editing, saving and downloading geometry​

The fields in a properties panel save when the user leaves the field (see Saving). Outlines are edited explicitly: Edit Geometry in the properties panel, or Edit selected feature in the toolbar, starts an edit, and Save or Cancel ends it; Escape cancels. Switching features with unsaved edits asks "Discard Unsaved Changes?" (Keep Editing or Discard Changes), and leaving the page asks for confirmation (Stay or Leave Page). The trash icon in the properties panel deletes a feature after confirmation.

Each feature downloads as GeoJSON from its tree row or panel, and the terrain and land cover as their files. The Model Geometry download saves all the geometry as one GeoJSON file, with the mesh (.h5, .hdf) once it is computed.

Changes to the geometry, terrain, land cover or mesh do not clear later results. Inundation and consequence results that depend on them are marked out of date instead, and are replaced by running them again.

Inundation Analysis​

The Inundation Analysis page, at Consequences > Inundation Analysis, defines and runs the flood simulations. An analysis is a named set of loading levels, which are reservoir water-surface elevations. Each loading level is simulated in HEC-RAS with the analysis's boundary conditions and plan settings, and the analysis's results are one flood map per loading level.

Figure shows the page with an analysis's dam boundary conditions open, and Table describes each numbered region.

1Analysis tilesOne tile per analysis, with its status dot and its Edit, Run, Copy and Delete icons, and the Add analysis tile.
2Boundary-condition listThe features of the active analysis that take a boundary condition.
3Simulation bandThe active analysis's run status, with a row per loading level and the icons for results, details and download.
4Boundary conditions panelThe selected feature's boundary conditions in the active analysis.

The page is a map (see The map) with these regions:

  • Analysis Configuration, a panel at the upper left, with a tile for each analysis, the boundary conditions of the selected analysis, and its simulation status.
  • [Feature] Boundary Conditions, a panel at the right that opens when a dam, model area, river or coastline is selected on the map or in the list. The geometry itself is read-only on this page.
  • The Depth over time at a point tool in the map toolbar, and the results layers in the legend.

Analyses​

Each analysis is a tile in the Analysis Configuration panel; selecting a tile makes it the active analysis. The icons on a tile are Edit analysis, Run the simulation, Copy analysis and Delete analysis. A status dot shows the last simulation: green "Simulated.", amber "Inputs have changed since the last simulation.", or red when the last simulation did not complete or could not start.

Add analysis creates an analysis named "Set N" and opens its Edit Analysis dialog. Copy analysis copies the plan settings, loading levels and boundary conditions into "[name] (Copy)". Delete analysis asks for confirmation; it deletes the analysis and all of its loading levels, and cancels a simulation that is running.

The Edit Analysis dialog holds the analysis name, its plan settings and its loading levels, and saves them with Save; Cancel discards the changes. Table lists its fields.

The Loading Level table lists the analysis's loading levels, one row each, with the columns Loading Level (the pool elevation, a positive number, unique in the analysis) and an optional Label of up to 100 characters. Removing a loading level deletes its results and its consequence runs.

Boundary conditions​

The panel below the tiles lists the features that can take a boundary condition in the active analysis: the dam centerline, the model area, the rivers that enter the model area, and the coastlines. Selecting one opens its boundary conditions at the right, under the heading "[feature] ([analysis])". Settings apply to every loading level of the analysis, except the values entered in tables with one row per loading level. The boundary conditions save as they are entered; there is no Save button.

Dam​

Boundary Condition Type is None (the default), Hydrograph or 1D Reservoir:

  • Hydrograph applies a dam-breach hydrograph directly to the 2D mesh. Its location is set with Latitude and Longitude, or with the crosshair button and a click on the dam centerline, and Width is the breach width over which it enters the mesh. Hydrograph Type and its tables are described in Hydrographs.
  • 1D Reservoir routes an inflow through a reservoir and a breach, on three tabs: Reservoir, Inflow and Breach.

The Reservoir tab states how the reservoir releases water: through the Stage-Outflow curve on the Operational Data page, or, without one, only over the dam crest. Storage & outflow curves shows the Operational Data curves the reservoir uses, read-only, with a link to edit them there. Its table gives, for each loading level, the Initial WSE (the reservoir level at the start of the simulation, defaulting to the loading level) and, when the outlet curve is in use, the Release at start. A red message explains any problem in the Stage-Outflow curve that stops a run.

The Inflow tab's Inflow Hydrograph check box routes an inflow hydrograph into the reservoir (see Hydrographs). The Breach tab's Breach check box turns the breach on.

Breach​

The breach fields appear on the Breach tab once Breach is checked. Table lists them. Breach Method decides where the breach geometry comes from: User Defined takes the development time, bottom width and side slopes for each loading level, and FEMA Defaults calculates them from the dam type, height and length (FEMA P-946, Table 9.3) for every loading level. Values entered under User Defined are kept while FEMA Defaults is selected.

The Breach Geometry plot draws the terrain along the dam, the dam crest, the breach location and each loading level's breach trapezoid, and the initial pipe for a piping failure. Under User Defined, the calculator icon opens the Breach Calculator, which estimates the breach with the MacDonald-Langridge-Monopolis or the Xu & Zhang / Von Thun method from the failure mode, the top of dam, breach bottom and pool elevations, the pool volume at failure, and the method's own dam inputs. Calculate shows the Calculator Results, and Apply Results writes them to every loading level.

Model area, rivers and coastlines​

  • Model area. Normal Depth Slope (default 0.001, greater than 0 and at most 1) is the outflow boundary that lets water leave the model area around its perimeter. The table's Initial water level for each loading level is left blank; the simulation service cannot yet start a 2D area wet, and a run with a value refuses to start.
  • River. Upstream Boundary is None or Hydrograph, an inflow series for the river. A river that crosses the dam takes its flow from the dam, and a river that starts inside the model area cannot take an inflow.
  • Coastline. Boundary is None or Hydrograph, a stage series applied along the model area edge between the ends of the coastline. Stages may be negative.

Hydrographs​

Hydrograph Type is Trapezoidal (the default) or Tabular. A trapezoidal hydrograph has, for each loading level, a Base value, a Base Time (hrs), a Peak value and a Peak Time (hrs): it rises from base to peak, holds the peak and returns to base by the base time, so the Peak Time must not exceed the Base Time. A tabular hydrograph has a tab for each loading level with Time (hr) and Value columns and at least two rows. Values are flows, or stages for a coastline. A chart plots one series per loading level.

Running an analysis​

The flood simulations are one of the two places in DST where the user starts a calculation (see Automatic Calculation); nothing runs on its own. Each analysis tile has a play button, Run the simulation (Run the simulation again once it has run), which simulates every loading level of that analysis. The button is unavailable, with the reason, when the analysis has no loading levels ("Add a loading level to run the analysis") or when simulations are not available. While the run is in progress the button becomes a spinner; selecting it cancels the run ("[status]. Click to cancel."). There is no separate retry: a failed or cancelled analysis is run again with the same button.

Before starting, DST checks the model and refuses to start with the reasons when, for example, the mesh is not computed, there is no terrain or land cover, no inflow is defined, or a breach is incomplete. Warnings that do not stop the run, such as a breach the reservoir never reaches, are reported as the run starts.

The [analysis] Simulation band under the boundary conditions shows the run's status: Waiting for a model server, Sending files, Queued, Building model, Running (with a percentage), Complete, Failed, Cancelled, Timed out or Lost. Its rows show each loading level, as Computed, Did not compute, Not run or Not verified, and its icons open the dam results, open Simulation details, and download the HEC-RAS project. When inputs change after a run, the status shows a warning: "Inputs have changed since this simulation ran. The depth grids show the earlier inputs; run the analysis again to update them."

Figure shows the details of a completed simulation.

Simulation details opens a dialog with the run's status, timing and HEC-RAS version; any reasons it could not start, errors, warnings and values built differently from what was asked; a Loading levels table with each level's result, volume error, largest cell error, water that left the model area and compute time; and folded sections for how the model was built and the console output. Download HEC-RAS project saves the project, results and logs as a zip file.

Results on the map​

The legend's Inundation Results group lists, for each analysis and loading level, Maximum depth, Depth over time and Water leaving the model. Turning on Depth over time shows a player with Play, Pause, the previous and next outputs, a Simulation time slider, a speed in simulation hours per second, Loop and a blend toggle. Each frame is the water surface at one mapping output time.

Depth over time at a point in the toolbar reads the depths at a point selected on the map. Its panel plots depth over time for each loading level of the chosen Analysis, with an Arrival depth (0.5 ft by default) and a table of when each level reaches that depth, its peak depth and time, and how long it stays that deep. Download CSV saves the depths.

Dam results​

The dam results dialog, [analysis] Results, opens from the chart icon in the simulation band or the Results button on the dam's boundary conditions. It lists, for each loading level, when overtopping and the breach started, when the breach was fully formed, the peak outflow and its time, the maximum pool and tailwater, and the water released. A time plot, Outflow through the dam, shows the outflow series the run kept, such as the flow through the breach, and the pool and tailwater, with Flow, Elevation and Both, Event and Whole simulation, and Compare levels; a cross-section of the breach follows the time bar. The table copies to the clipboard and downloads as CSV.

Figure shows the dam results of an analysis with a piping breach.

Changing an input after a run does not clear its results; the analysis is marked out of date until it is run again.

Structure inventory​

Structures opens the Add Structure Inventory dialog, with two tabs:

  • National Structure Inventory downloads the USACE National Structure Inventory (NSI) structures inside the model area. It needs a model area.
  • Upload File reads a GeoJSON FeatureCollection of points with NSI attribute names, up to 2 GB, chosen with Select GeoJSON File.

Name is optional; a blank name is generated. Replacing an inventory deletes the old one and any map edits to its structures once the new one is read. DST reports "Structure inventory "[name]" is ready ([N] structures)." when it finishes.

The inventory's properties panel shows its Name, its details (source, file, import date, number of structures and the structures edited since the import, listed by View Edits), and two cards:

  • LifeSim checks counts the structures with a problem, such as "Outside the Emergency Response area", "Missing population", "Missing occupancy type", "Missing foundation height" or "More people than the occupancy holds". Show colors those structures and zooms to them. These are warnings; they do not stop a run, though LifeSim leaves out structures outside the Emergency Response area.
  • Summary gives the day and night population, under 65 and 65 and over, the total value, and the structures counted by a characteristic chosen in Structures by (damage category by default).

Undo reverses the last change, and Save or Cancel ends the edit. A new point starts with the nearest structure's attributes. Table lists a structure's fields.

The Emergency Response rates how well the people in the model area would be warned and would evacuate. It always exists, and its area is the model area. Table lists its fields. The ratings save when chosen, and the notes when the user leaves the box.

Non-failure hazard zone​

The non-failure hazard zone is the part of the model area that floods even if the dam does not fail. On the Consequence Analysis page it gets its own hazard occurrence and advanced notice. A screening has at most one. Non-Failure Zone opens the Add Non-Failure Hazard Zone dialog, with three tabs:

  • Draw on Map: Start Drawing, then a click at each corner; a double-click or Enter finishes.
  • Import a File: a GeoJSON file of polygons in longitude and latitude (WGS 84), up to 50 MB and 200,000 points. All its polygons become one zone.
  • Inundation Result: the wet cells of one Loading level's maximum depth grid, widened by a Buffer distance (100 ft by default, from 0 to 5,000 ft) and cut to the model area.

The zone's properties panel shows its area and source. Edit Geometry reshapes an outline of one piece without holes; any other outline is changed by replacing the zone. Deleting the zone also removes its hazard occurrence and advanced notice.

Consequence Analysis​

The Consequence Analysis page, at Consequences > Consequence Analysis, sets when the hazard occurs and how much notice people have at each loading level, and runs a LifeSim simulation for each loading level of each analysis. It lists every analysis from the Inundation Analysis page; with none, it reads "Nothing to run yet" and links to Open Inundation Analysis.

Figure shows the page with one analysis expanded, and Table describes each numbered region. Step 2, Set the advanced notice, lies below the hazard table, out of view in the figure. The non-failure hazard zone map, shown above the Run panel, appears only when the screening has a zone.

The page has three steps: Confirm when the hazard occurs and Set the advanced notice on the left, and Run on the right. Settings save as they are chosen; typed times and text save when the user leaves the field (see Saving).

Each analysis is a card that opens to list its loading levels, with an All levels row that sets every level at once.

Set the advanced notice​

The advanced notice is how long before the hazard occurs the threat is identified: Delayed (−2 to 0 h), Very short (0–2 h), Short (2–4 h), Moderate (4–8 h), Long (8–24 h) or Very long (24–48 h). It has no default, and an analysis cannot run until it is chosen. It is set for each analysis, and Set by loading level sets a level differently. When a zone exists, the zone has one notice for every analysis. Add the basis records why the notice was chosen, up to 2000 characters.

Run​

The Run panel states how many loading levels are ready to run, and what keeps the others from running, with a link to the page that fixes it. The consequence simulations, like the flood simulations, start only when the user starts them. The run controls are:

  • Run all (Run all [N] when some levels are ready), at the top of the panel, runs every ready loading level that has no current result. It is unavailable when no level is ready, or when every ready level is already running or current.
  • Run, Run 1 level or Run [N] levels, beside each analysis, runs that analysis's ready levels.
  • The play button beside each loading level runs that level again, whatever its status.
  • Cancel or Cancel [N] cancels the runs in progress, at the top or beside an analysis, and the stop button beside a level cancels that level.

None of these asks for confirmation. The panel lists each level's mean life loss, with warning and evacuation, or its status: Not run, Queued, Simulating, Writing results, Failed, Cancelled, Could not start, or the reason it is blocked. A result marked Out of date was simulated with inputs that have since changed.

Selecting a level's status opens its details: the run's status and timing, any errors and warnings, the result over its 1,000 iterations (mean, median, 5th to 95th percentile and range of life loss, the mean population at risk and the mean damages), and the hazard and notice it ran with.

Consequence functions​

A card at the top represents each function. One function can be the non-failure function, chosen with its Non-fail button; every other function is a failure function. Add function adds a function entered as tables, with a Name (up to 100 characters, unique) and a Role (Failure or Non-failure); such a function can be renamed or deleted from its card. Download all (.csv) saves every function's table as the risk analysis receives it.

Graphs and tables​

Graphs shows the function's Life loss and Economic damages against stage: life loss as a box per loading level with its mean, damages as bars by damage category. Notes below say which levels are left out and why, such as a level that is out of date or not run, and how the function is held beyond its lowest and highest stages.

Tables lists the rows the risk analysis receives. Table lists the columns. DST's rows, from the loading levels, are gray and read-only. User-defined rows can be added, pasted and edited like any DST table (see Tables); they sort by stage and save as they are entered, and a user row at a level's stage is sent in place of the level's. Changes are not saved while any cell is in error.

Loading level summary and plots​

For a function from an analysis, the Loading level selector picks one level. Summary shows its mean life loss and 5–95% range, mean damages, population at risk, life loss ÷ population at risk, first water and the share warned and leaving, the inputs it Ran with, and its Damages by category.
